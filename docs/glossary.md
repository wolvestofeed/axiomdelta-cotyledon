# Glossary

The vocabulary of Axiom Delta Wellness Center's microgreens and sprouts program, in three tiers. Tiers 1 and 2 are published for subscribers and staff. Tier 3 is the operating system's own language and stays internal. Source numbers refer to rows in [`science-library.md`](science-library.md).

This file is the vocabulary authority for Phase 2, the way `outline.md` §3 was for Phase 1.

---

## Tier 1 — Growing and the product (subscribers and staff)

**Microgreen.** A vegetable, herb or grain seedling harvested 7 to 21 days after sowing, when the seed leaves have opened and the first true leaves are starting. Cut above the root line; you eat stem and leaves. Needs light and a growing medium. [2, 4]

**Sprout.** A germinated seed grown 2 to 10 days in the dark or low light with no medium. You eat the whole thing: seed, root and shoot. Higher moisture and slightly more protein than a microgreen of the same seed, about half the fiber. [2, 3, 10]

**Live tray.** A tray of microgreens still growing when it reaches you. You water it and harvest what you need each day. This is the program's main product.

**Cut microgreens.** Harvested and packed by weight, sold by the ounce.

**Flat.** The standard 10 by 20 inch growing tray, called a 1020. The largest unit sold.

**Tray format.** The size a variety is grown in: 1020 flat, 7 by 11 large tray, 5 by 5 insert, pint jar for sprouts, or cut ounce.

**Variety.** The specific seed: Di Cicco broccoli, black oil sunflower, speckled pea, red garnet amaranth. A variety carries its seed source, organic status, origin, cost, yield and nutrient profile. Everything in the program starts from the variety.

**Blend.** Two or more varieties grown together in one tray, composed to a nutrition target and named for the benefits it is grown for.

**Nutrition target.** What a subscriber wants more of: iron, protein, vitamin C, sulforaphane, fiber. A flat plan is composed against it.

**Flat plan.** What each distribution of a subscription carries: the grow plans and how many units of each, kept as dated versions so a change takes effect from the next distribution not yet sown.

**Subscription.** A subscriber's standing order at one pickup point: weekly, every two weeks, or monthly on the same weekday of the same week of the month, from its first distribution, with its flat plan. A distribution is skipped, or the subscription paused, only before the distribution's sow date.

**Pickup point.** Where a subscriber collects: the farm, a shared location, or their own address on a delivery route.

**Harvest window.** The days a live tray is distributed in and cut greens are harvested from it, after the cotyledons are full and the first true leaves appear. A finished tray is stock for its window and waste after it.

**Cotyledon.** The seed leaves, the first pair to open. Microgreens are harvested at the cotyledon stage or just after.

**True leaf.** The first leaf with the plant's adult shape, after the cotyledons. Its appearance marks harvest time for microgreens.

**Hypocotyl.** The stem between root and seed leaves. In a sprout you eat it; in a microgreen it is most of what you cut.

**Radicle.** The first root out of the seed.

**Germination.** The seed waking up: taking on water, splitting, sending out the radicle. Enzymes break stored starch and protein into forms the seedling and you can use. [3, 9]

**Soak.** Large seeds (sunflower, pea, wheat) are soaked 6 to 12 hours before sowing so they germinate evenly.

**Blackout.** Days after sowing when the tray is covered and dark so the seedlings stretch up before they see light. One to four days depending on variety.

**Weighted germination.** A tray placed on top of the sown seed for the first days so roots go down and stems come up straight.

**Bottom watering.** Once roots grow through the perforated tray, water goes in the tray underneath and the roots draw it up. Keeps leaves dry.

**Misting.** Spraying seed and seedlings from above during germination and blackout, before roots reach the bottom.

**Growing medium.** What the seed sits in: coconut coir, jute fiber mat, hemp mat, vermiculite, peat, or a hydroponic pad. The medium changes nitrate, dry matter and antioxidant levels in the crop. [8]

**Hemp mat.** A needle-punched mat of hemp fiber, the program's default medium. No binders or chemical treatment; pH-neutral and salt-free, so it needs no buffering or rinsing; holds its water inside the fiber. [94, 97]

**Coconut coir.** Shredded coconut husk fiber. Holds water, drains, and composts.

**Jute fiber.** A woven plant-fiber mat used as a soil-free medium. Some varieties, such as red basil, express more antioxidants on jute and vermiculite. [8]

**Hydroponic.** Grown on an inert pad with nutrients in the water rather than in a soil-like medium.

**Nutrient solution.** Minerals added to the water at set stages for specific varieties. Named per variety on its grow plan.

**Light spectrum.** The mix of wavelengths a grow light emits: full-spectrum white, red and blue, far-red. Varieties are grown under different spectra, and spectrum changes color, flavor and nutrient content. [2, 8]

**Photoperiod.** Hours of light per day during the light stage; 16 hours is the program's baseline.

**PPFD.** Photosynthetic photon flux density: how much usable light reaches the tray, in micromoles per square meter per second. Set by the fixture and its height above the tray.

**Light stage.** The days after blackout when trays are under the grow lights until harvest.

**Daily light integral (DLI).** The total light a tray receives in a day: PPFD times hours, in moles per square meter. The figure the energy cost and the biofortification claims hang off. [75]

**Red-to-blue ratio.** The mix of red and blue in a grow light. Red grows mass; blue grows compounds. 5:1 is the studied balance for broccoli; blue-heavy mixes trade some weight for more antioxidants. [68]

**Far-red.** Light just past visible red, 700 to 800 nm. Read by the plant as shade, it stretches stems and, at low intensity, sharply raises vitamin C and glucosinolates in broccoli. [73]

**Continuous light.** Lights on 24 hours. Raised weight and antioxidant enzymes in brassica microgreens without visible damage inside the short harvest window. [75]

**UV-C pulse.** A brief ultraviolet exposure, about 10 minutes, that doubles chlorophyll and raises carotenoids as the plant defends its photosystems. [74]

**Light regime.** A named light recipe on a grow plan: ratio, far-red share, intensity, hours, and any UV-C pulse. Yield, balanced, nutrition-forward, biofortify, and continuous are the catalog.

**Cocopeat.** Coconut coir fiber. pH 5.5 to 7.0, 90 to 95% porosity, naturally antifungal, more fresh weight than soil. [65]

**Electrical conductivity (EC).** The strength of a nutrient solution, measured by how well it conducts. Set on the nutrient line for hydroponic plans.

**Damping-off.** Seedlings collapsing at the soil line from fungus in a wet, airless medium. Aeration and cocopeat's antifungal action prevent it. [2]

**Organic seed.** Seed certified grown without synthetic pesticides or fertilizers. Recorded on the variety.

**Heirloom.** An open-pollinated variety passed down for generations, not a hybrid. Recorded on the variety.

**Seed sanitation.** Treating seed before soaking to reduce pathogens that live inside the seed coat, where surface washing does not reach. Required for sprouts. [17, 50, 51]

**Lot.** One sowing of one variety on one day, tracked from seed bag to your tray so any question can be traced back.

## Tier 2 — The science (subscribers who want it, and staff)

### Nutrition basics

**Nutrient density.** How much nutrition per gram. Microgreens run 4 to 40 times the density of the mature vegetable, depending on species and what is measured. [4]

**Fresh weight (FW).** Measured as harvested, water included. All the per-100-gram figures in the library are fresh weight.

**Bioavailability.** How much of a nutrient your body can actually absorb and use. Germination raises it by breaking down the compounds that lock minerals up. [3]

**Antinutrient.** A compound in a raw seed that blocks mineral absorption: phytic acid, tannins, oxalates, trypsin inhibitors. Sprouting degrades them, which is why sprouted legumes deliver more calcium, iron and zinc than the dry seed. [3]

**Hidden hunger.** Micronutrient deficiency in people who get enough calories. The condition microgreens are proposed as an answer to. [3]

**Functional food.** A food eaten for a health effect beyond basic nutrition. The category the research puts microgreens and sprouts in.

**Macronutrient.** Protein, carbohydrate, fat and fiber. Legume microgreens (lentil, mung bean) are the protein and carbohydrate leaders. [9]

**Micronutrient.** Vitamins and minerals needed in small amounts.

**Trace mineral.** A mineral needed in tiny amounts: iron, zinc, manganese, copper, selenium. Broccoli microgreens lead in iron and manganese; pea in copper. [14]

**Macro-mineral.** Calcium, magnesium, potassium, phosphorus. Sunflower leads in calcium. [14]

**Dietary fiber.** Indigestible plant carbohydrate that feeds gut bacteria and slows sugar absorption. Microgreens carry about twice the fiber of sprouts. [10]

**Prebiotic.** Fiber that feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

**Essential fatty acid.** Fats the body cannot make. Microgreen fat is low in amount but high in alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3) and linoleic acid (omega-6). [2]

**Ascorbic acid.** Vitamin C. Water-soluble antioxidant and essential vitamin. Bean, pea and sunflower microgreens carry the most; the transition to light-fed growth drives its synthesis. [10, 14]

### Plant compounds

**Phytochemical.** A compound a plant makes for its own defense, color or signaling that also acts in the human body. The broad term for everything below.

**Secondary metabolite.** A phytochemical not needed for the plant's basic growth: pigments, defense compounds, flavors.

**Polyphenol.** A large family of plant compounds with antioxidant activity. Measured as gallic acid equivalents (GAE). Broccoli and buckwheat microgreens are the leaders. [9, 14]

**Flavonoid.** A subgroup of polyphenols that includes rutin, quercetin, vitexin, orientin and the anthocyanins. Measured as rutin equivalents. [16]

**Rutin.** A flavonoid concentrated in buckwheat that supports capillary strength and blood pressure regulation. [16, 49]

**Quercetin.** A flavonoid in buckwheat and many greens with antioxidant activity. [16]

**Anthocyanin.** The red and purple pigment in red cabbage and purple radish. An antioxidant that also modulates fat metabolism. Measured as cyanidin-3-glucoside equivalents (CGE). [9, 31]

**Carotenoid.** The orange and yellow pigments: beta-carotene, lutein. Fat-soluble antioxidants that protect the eye and nervous system. Kale and Sango radish lead. [15]

**Lutein.** A carotenoid concentrated in the retina; associated with eye health. [15]

**Beta-carotene.** A carotenoid the body converts to vitamin A. [15]

**Chlorophyll.** The green pigment of photosynthesis. Lentil microgreens are exceptionally high. [9]

**Organic acid.** Small acids that shape flavor and cellular metabolism: citric (red beet), succinic (bean), fumaric (sunflower). [14]

**Glucosinolate.** The sulfur compounds of the cabbage family (brassicas). Inert in the intact plant; converted to active compounds when cut or chewed. [22]

**Glucoraphanin.** The glucosinolate in broccoli that becomes sulforaphane. Peaks in 3-day-old broccoli sprouts at 10 to 100 times the level of mature broccoli. [23, 25]

**Myrosinase.** The plant enzyme, held in separate cells from glucoraphanin, that converts it to sulforaphane when tissue is damaged. Chewing fresh sprouts releases it; cooking destroys it. [22, 27]

**Isothiocyanate.** The active compound class myrosinase produces from glucosinolates. Sulforaphane is one.

**Sulforaphane.** The isothiocyanate from broccoli sprouts, the most-studied compound in this field. Activates the body's own antioxidant and detoxification genes rather than acting as an antioxidant itself. [19, 22]

### How it works in the body

**Antioxidant.** A compound that neutralizes reactive oxygen species. Direct antioxidants (vitamin C, carotenoids) do it themselves. Indirect antioxidants (sulforaphane) switch on the body's own systems.

**Reactive oxygen species (ROS).** Unstable oxygen molecules, "free radicals", produced by metabolism and stress that damage cells, fats and DNA.

**Oxidative stress.** More ROS than the body's defenses can clear. Implicated in aging, cardiovascular disease, diabetes and neurological conditions.

**Nrf2.** A protein that, when released, enters the cell nucleus and switches on more than 200 protective genes. The master switch sulforaphane throws. [19, 21]

**Keap1.** The protein that holds Nrf2 inactive in the cell. Sulforaphane binds Keap1 and frees Nrf2. [21]

**Antioxidant Response Element (ARE).** The stretch of DNA Nrf2 binds to start protective gene expression. The pathway is written Keap1-Nrf2-ARE.

**Phase II enzyme.** Detoxification enzymes that tag harmful compounds for excretion. Sulforaphane is a potent inducer. [22, 25]

**Glutathione.** The body's main internal antioxidant. Nrf2 raises its production and recycling. [19, 21]

**Chemoprevention.** Using a food or compound to lower the chance of cancer developing. The Phase II enzyme effect of broccoli sprouts is the studied mechanism. [25, 47]

**Lipid peroxidation.** Oxidative damage to fats in cell membranes and blood. Measured by malondialdehyde and oxidized LDL. [29]

**LDL and HDL.** Low-density and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, the "bad" and "good" fractions. Red cabbage microgreens lowered LDL 34% in a mouse model. [31]

**Advanced glycation end products (AGEs).** Damaged proteins formed when sugar binds them, linked to vascular aging. Microgreen extracts reduced their formation in lab studies. [35]

**Insulin resistance and HOMA-IR.** The body's reduced response to insulin, and the standard index that measures it. Broccoli sprout powder lowered both in a human trial. [38]

**Type 2 diabetes (T2DM).** Chronic high blood sugar from insulin resistance. The condition in the Bahadoran trial. [38]

**Metabolic syndrome.** The cluster of high blood pressure, high blood sugar, excess abdominal fat and abnormal cholesterol.

**AMPK.** An enzyme that regulates cellular energy; one of the pathways sulforaphane acts through. [42]

**Pentose phosphate pathway.** A glucose-processing route that produces NADPH, the fuel for making glutathione. Sulforaphane redirects glucose toward it. [21]

**Neuroprotection.** Protecting nerve cells from damage. Sulforaphane crosses the blood-brain barrier; human evidence is strongest in autism, preclinical elsewhere. [19, 20, 43]

**Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD).** The condition in the Johns Hopkins trial, where 18 weeks of sulforaphane improved behavior scales and the gains reversed on stopping. [43]

**H. pylori.** The stomach bacterium behind gastritis and ulcers. Sulforaphane kills it in the lab and reduces colonization in people. [46, 48]

### Measuring

**DPPH, ABTS, FRAP, ORAC.** Laboratory assays of antioxidant capacity. Different tests, not interchangeable; a claim names its assay. [4, 9]

**GAE, RE, CGE.** Gallic acid, rutin and cyanidin-3-glucoside equivalents: the units polyphenols, flavonoids and anthocyanins are reported in.

**mg/100 g FW and µg/100 g FW.** Milligrams or micrograms per 100 grams fresh weight, the standard unit for nutrient content.

**In vivo, in vitro, murine model, cell model.** In a living body, in a dish, in mice, in cultured cells. A result from a mouse or a dish is stated as that, never as a human result.

**Randomized controlled trial (RCT).** People assigned by chance to treatment or placebo. The strongest single-study evidence. Double-blind means neither side knows who got which.

**Meta-analysis.** A statistical pooling of many trials. [37]

**Phase 1 trial.** A first human safety study, not an efficacy study. [28]

**Biofortification.** Raising a crop's nutrient content deliberately through growing conditions: light spectrum, mild stress, substrate, microbial inoculation. [2, 8]

**Elicitor.** A stress the plant answers by making defense compounds: blue light, far-red, UV, continuous light, a resistive medium, a nutrient shortfall. The compounds it makes to survive are the ones that act in the body. [62, 64]

**Phytochrome.** The plant's red and far-red sensor. Activated by red, it drives stem and leaf growth; a low red-to-far-red ratio triggers shade avoidance. [68]

**Cryptochrome.** The plant's blue and ultraviolet sensor. Blue light through cryptochrome keeps growth compact and switches on the phenylpropanoid pathway. [68]

**Phenylpropanoid pathway.** The metabolic route that builds phenolics, flavonoids and anthocyanins. Blue light turns it up. [68]

**Shade avoidance.** The stretch response to far-red: the plant reads a dense neighbor and grows taller. Useful at low intensity, leggy past it. [73]

**Dilution effect.** In field crops, breeding and fertilizing for weight lowers the concentration of minerals and phytochemicals. Controlled-environment microgreens escape it by harvesting before the compounds are spent. [62, 64]

**Cation exchange capacity (CEC).** A medium's ability to hold and release nutrient ions. High in cocopeat. [65]

**Porosity.** The share of a medium that is air and water space. Cocopeat runs 90 to 95%, which is why roots breathe in it. [65]

### Safety

**Good Agricultural Practices (GAP).** The standard set of growing, handling and sanitation practices for produce safety. [54]

**Salmonella and STEC.** The pathogens behind sprout outbreaks. STEC is Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli*. Warm, wet, dark sprout conditions favor them, and seed can carry them internally. [17, 50, 51]

**Produce Safety Rule.** The FDA rule under FSMA that covers growing, harvesting, packing and holding produce. Sprouts have their own subpart with seed treatment and spent-water testing.

**PGPR.** Plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria, beneficial microbes inoculated onto seed or substrate to crowd out pathogens and raise yield. [55]

**UV-C treatment.** Post-harvest ultraviolet exposure studied for pathogen reduction on microgreens. [53]

## Tier 3 — Operating vocabulary (internal)

**Grow plan.** The record of how a variety, or a blend, is grown: seed lines, medium line, nutrient lines, light line, tray format, and the stage schedule. Every line feeds the costing formula. Replaces the kitchen's recipe.

**Seed line.** A line on a grow plan naming a variety and its grams per tray by format, and its share of a blend. The costing basis.

**Medium line.** The growing medium and quantity per tray on a grow plan.

**Nutrient line.** A nutrient solution, its concentration, and the stage it starts, on a grow plan.

**Light line.** The light spec on a grow plan: spectrum, photoperiod, PPFD or fixture height, and the stage it starts. Costed as the shelf's fixtures at full power per tray per day plus their amortized cost; any lit shelf takes it.

**Fixture.** A grow light on a grow unit: model, spectrum, watts, and its PPFD map at tray height.

**Consumable.** A material costed per tray by format rather than planned per line: tray sets, labels, care inserts, sanitizer.

**Stage.** One step of the grow cycle with a duration, a watering method and a labor basis: soak, sow, germination, blackout, light, harvest window, packed.

**Stage schedule.** The ordered stages of a grow plan with their days. Drives the grow calendar and the labor plan.

**Stage record.** What happened at a stage on a sowing: dates, temperature and humidity readings, control-point checks.

**Control point.** A check that must be recorded: seed sanitation before soak, spent-water test for sprouts, temperature and humidity in germination and light.

**Sowing.** One sow of one grow plan on one day. The production batch, the costing basis, and one lot per variety. Only a closed sowing posts journals.

**Unit.** One of whatever a grow plan produces: a flat, tray, insert, jar or cut ounce, per its format.

**Grow unit.** A shelf, rack, sprouting rack or jar stand, lit or dark. A sowing is the trays its orders need; a sowing larger than one unit holds is split across units.

**Sowing stream.** Labor counted per sowing on the sow day: soak, sow, weight.

**Daily stream.** Labor counted per tray per day across the cycle: misting, bottom watering, inspection.

**Harvest stream.** Labor counted per unit on the distribution day: harvest, pack, hand-off.

**Yield chain.** Seed weight in, sown weight after soak, harvest weight out, packed weight or unit count. Replaces the kitchen's as-purchased to plated chain.

**Last price paid.** A variety's price per pound on its most recent accepted receipt. The plan's price for its seed line.

**Rolling 12-month average.** The dollars over the quantity on the accepted receipt lines of the last twelve months, per variety, medium and nutrient. A key figure; no plan, order or posting uses it.

**Yield per pound.** Observed units, or harvest grams, per pound of seed by format, from closed sowings. The other half of the cost basis.

**Standard.** An approved, effective-dated version of a grow plan's labor standard, its variable overhead per tray and the fixed overhead rate. A sowing with no crew recorded takes its labor from it; materials are always at the cost of the lots drawn.

**Plan of record.** The one saved forecast the workspace reports against.

**Forecast.** Any saved scenario: business planning over the data already in the system, with labor, costs, packaging, transport and prices adjusted to see revenue and profit. Only "forecast" appears on screen.

**R&D.** The section where new grow plans, blends first, are composed and tested before they go in service.

**Experiment.** A titled run in R&D: one sowing of a developing grow plan, recording harvest grams per variety, the stage checks and its time study. Its cost is Research and Development, not inventory.

**Plan and Actual.** The two ledgers: the forecast's own run, and the recorded facts.

**Workspace.** A farm. Every table carries a workspace id.

**Subscriber.** A customer on any channel: subscription, restaurant, retail and wholesale.

**Distribution.** Pickup or delivery on a distribution day.

**Tray return.** A live tray's set coming back for reuse; tracked per subscriber.
