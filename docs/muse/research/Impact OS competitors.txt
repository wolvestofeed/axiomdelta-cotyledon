A detailed pricing breakdown covers subscription fees, onboarding/implementation costs, and volume or license constraints for the four platforms:

---

### 1. Wherefour (Food Safety & Traceability ERP)

*Custom-quoted SaaS tailored to production complexity and compliance requirements.*

* **Subscription / Monthly Fees:**
* Typically starts around **$600 to $1,500/month** for small-to-mid commercial food operations.
* Highly variable based on feature sets (e.g., automated batch scaling, FSMA 204 module, API access, integrations).


* **Setup & Implementation Costs:**
* Usually ranges from **$2,500 to $7,500** for onboarding, recipe/BOM imports, mobile barcode scanner setup, and staff training.
* Significantly more accessible than traditional enterprise food ERPs (like JustFoodERP or BatchMaster), which often demand $25k–$50k+ up-front.


* **Terms & Capacity Limits:**
* Quoted per facility and transaction volume (order or batch run tiers). User seats are often either bundled in blocks or billed on a per-user basis (~$50/user/mo for floor workers vs. admin users).



---

### 2. Brahmin Solutions (Cloud MRP)

*Transparent, revenue-bracketed pricing that eliminates the per-seat penalty on kitchen floor staff.*

* **Subscription / Monthly Fees:**
* **$199/month** (billed annually) for businesses with **under $1M** in annual gross revenue.
* **$499/month** (billed annually) for businesses with **$1M to $10M** in annual revenue.
* Month-to-month billing is available at a ~15–20% premium.


* **Setup & Implementation Costs:**
* **$0 to $1,500** (Brahmin is self-service friendly with guided onboarding calls and automated CSV template imports for inventory, suppliers, and multi-tier BOMs).


* **Terms & Capacity Limits:**
* **Unlimited users** on standard plans (a massive advantage for a 20-person kitchen where you want multiple prep cooks and packers scanning without paying per seat).
* Tier limits are governed primarily by **annual revenue brackets** and total monthly sales order/shipment volume.



---

### 3. FlexiBake / Streamline ERP (Bakery & Commissary Specialist)

*Tiered modular software designed around standing orders, commercial batches, and route delivery.*

* **Subscription / Monthly Fees:**
* **Base Tier:** ~$295/month.
* **Professional Tier:** ~$375/month.
* **Corporate Tier:** ~$495/month.
* **Enterprise / Streamline ERP:** Call for pricing (typically $800–$1,500+/mo for complex distribution/multi-facility).


* **Setup & Implementation Costs:**
* Ranges from **$1,500 to $5,000** depending on the level of dedicated consultant hours purchased for recipe ingestion, custom route sheet templates, and nutritional panel layout setup.


* **Terms & Capacity Limits:**
* User tiers are usually restricted to **concurrency** or specific named users (e.g., 2–5 included users, additional seats running ~$50–$100/mo).
* Lower tiers may restrict advanced features like direct routing/driver logistics, e-commerce integrations, or advanced FDA/CFIA nutrition label generators (often sold as add-on modules).



---

### 4. Odoo (Enterprise Edition)

*Modular open-core platform offering an all-in-one software footprint at the cost of custom configuration.*

* **Subscription / Monthly Fees:**
* **Standard Plan:** ~$24.90 to $31.10 per user/month (gives access to all standard apps: MRP, Inventory, Purchase, Invoicing, POS).
* **Custom Plan:** ~$37.40 to $46.80 per user/month (required if you want multi-company, API integrations to delivery aggregators, or developer hosting via Odoo.sh).
* *Example (5 admin/office seats):* ~$150 – $200/month. Floor workers can often be tracked via shared barcode kiosk terminals.


* **Setup & Implementation Costs:**
* **Variable:** Self-implemented via Odoo Success Packs ($2,000 to $4,500), or outsourced to an Odoo implementation partner ($7,500 to $20,000+) to properly map food yield losses, cook-chill shelf-life expirations, and standing school contract workflows.


* **Terms & Capacity Limits:**
* **No transaction or order caps.** You pay strictly for named users.
* Maintenance costs can compound if you over-customize with custom Python code rather than standard modules, leading to migration headaches during version upgrades.
