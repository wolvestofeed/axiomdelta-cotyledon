## Positioning decision (important)
The demo is branded **"Mise Commissary"** with a demo tenant labeled **"Central Texas Commissary"**,
not the source company, which is in stealth; a page naming it, its phases, its school program and its
unit economics is a real exposure risk even on an unlisted link. Robert can flip the tenant label to
the company's name for the call if he chooses. It was NOT published to getcomptable.com for the same
reason, and because building the company's branded product on his own domain stakes a visible claim
while the IP question is open.

## What is genuinely live and calculated
- **Recipes & Cost** — twelve ingredient lines, AP quantity, yield to cooked, editable unit price.
  Change any price and the entire platform recosts.
- **Production Plan** — the spine. A forecast slider drives batches, labor hours, direct labor cost,
  a full purchase order with case rounding and lead times, and the day's journal entries.
- **Ledger** — six double-entry journal entries generated from production and payroll, a trial
  balance, and an annualised contribution P&L. Debits and credits balance and the page proves it.
- **Labor & Payroll** — fourteen-task time study, throughput metrics, payroll accrual with employer
  tax split matching what posts to the ledger.
- **Food Safety** — five CCPs with real critical limits, and a cooling log where pass/fail is
  evaluated on the record, not at review.
- **Training** — module library with completion, and one wired CCP-2 assessment question.
- **Today** — three scenarios (pilot 125/day, phase 1, full build) with live phase economics and a
  facility utilisation chart by time window.

## What is a designed static surface (labeled as such in the app)
Procurement supplier register, Menu Cycle, Sites & Customers, Inventory & Cold Storage. The analysis
inside them is real; the data is illustrative.

## The Platform Map module
A twelve-row honest accounting of every module: what is live, what came from Robert's shipped products
(GetComptable, the sequencing engine), what is designed but not built, and what effort remains. This is
the anti-overclaim device. It exists so nothing in the demo is mistaken for more than it is, and so the
procurement module is explicitly marked "the real build, weeks not days."

## The argument the demo makes
Back office solved on day one means the operator's attention goes to the only question that decides
whether the business works: mapping regenerative supply to volume, holding time and cold storage at a
cost the meal can carry. The platform buys the time. It is not the thing that wins.
