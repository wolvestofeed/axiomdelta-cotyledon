Plan, Forecasts and Compare

How the OS separates what is recorded from what is planned, how a chef edits a plan and keeps it as a forecast, and how the Compare page places one day under two forecasts or costs one recipe against another.

01The words the platform uses

Plan of record
The one saved forecast the whole workspace reports against. The forecast bar names it: Plan of record: <name>. An admin sets it. When none is set, the plan-data defaults stand in.
Forecast
Any saved set of edits to the plan. Open one and every page renders it until you return to the plan of record. The bar then reads Forecast: <name>.
Working copy
Your unsaved edits in this browser. Pages that recompute live show them; a page built on the server shows the last saved version and says so.
Plan
The world where the open forecast runs: its orders, batches, purchases and deliveries, posted as the Plan ledger. Nothing is recorded on Plan.
Actual
The world of recorded facts: batch records, receipts, deliveries, bills, payments, punches. Recording happens only here.
Figure tags
Every figure carries where it came from: Stated typed by a person, Sourced cited to a document or catalog line, Derived computed by the engine, Placeholder a working figure with no source yet.

02Plan and Actual: the toggle in the forecast bar

The forecast bar is the top bar on every page of the OS. At its left are two buttons, Plan and Actual. The one pressed is the world every figure on the page reads from.

  • Plan shows the open forecast’s own run, unsaved edits included. Change an input and the statements move with it.
  • Actual shows only what has been recorded. With no records in a period, every Actual figure is zero.

Some surfaces do not follow the toggle:

  • The Floor and Food Safety always read Actual.
  • The Parent Portal reads the records only.
  • CRM follows the open forecast.

Inventory, Plan v Actual and HR pick their copy by the world selected; the first line of each page’s How this page works panel names the world it describes.

03Editing a plan and keeping it as a forecast

  1. Switch to Plan in the forecast bar. Edits are made on Plan; Actual is for recording.
  2. Edit an input on a page that carries them: an ingredient’s price or quantity on Recipes, a vessel capacity on Equipment, an operating hour on Capacity, a service’s meals per service on Customers, a step’s minutes on Process. Every dependent figure recomputes as you type.
  3. Read the section bar. The edited section shows Unsaved changes in <section> with two controls: Revert <section> discards the edits; Save as forecast opens a name field. Type a name and press Save. The forecast is created and opened, and the bar reads Forecast: <name>.
  4. With a forecast open, the bar carries the rest: Save forecast writes your working copy back to it; Save as new forecast forks it under a new name; Revert to saved drops unsaved edits; Back to plan of record closes it.
  5. Open a saved one at any time from Open forecast…. The list marks the plan of record.
  6. Admins only: Set as plan of record makes the open forecast the one the workspace reports against. Each change posts to the trail with the forecast as applied, so a past month compares with the plan in force at its end.
Two things a forecast never does A forecast never changes a customer record, a recipe in the library, a time study or any recorded document: it holds edits on top of them. And no planning page ever writes to the ledger; only a closed batch record, a receipt, a delivery, a bill or a payment posts a journal.

04Compare — the Day tab

Production Planning → Compare opens on Day: the same day placed under two forecasts, side by side. Each side resolves its own forecast, rolls the order book through production and places the day with the same scheduler.

  1. In the page toolbar, pick Forecast A and Forecast B. The list holds every saved forecast, the plan of record marked, and Plan-data defaults. Picking the same forecast on both sides makes every row read the same.
  2. Pick the Day. The list is every date either forecast produces or delivers on in the next three weeks.
  3. Read the four tiles: portions and batches placed per side, and each side’s operating day with its makespan.
  4. Read the table. Each row is a fact off the placed day: portions and batches placed, batches unplaced, portions shipped, the binding resource, makespan, crew hours, idle crew hours, labor minutes and labor minutes per portion, closedown hours, chiller utilisation, and the count of findings. The Δ column is B minus A. A bold Δ marks a row where more or less is plainly the direction for meals placed and crew time used; every other row is a fact, not a score.
  5. Read Findings the two days differ on: the finding kinds one side raises and the other does not, such as a unit over capacity, a crew short, the cooling clock, a chill completing unattended, a delivery time missed.

Labor on the Day tab is minutes, never dollars: the scheduler carries no wage, and pay is held in CompTable. Each day can be opened on the Day Schedule, and the month either forecast makes on the Calendar.

05Compare — the Recipe tab (admins)

The Recipe tab costs two recipes under the open forecast: a library recipe against a variant built from an instruction you type or dictate. The model turns the instruction into a typed proposal; the engine finds, derives or asks for every figure the variant needs; nothing is costed until the proposal is clean, and nothing on the table comes from the model.

Describe the change

  1. Pick Recipe A in the toolbar. This is the source recipe the variant starts from.
  2. Type the change in the box, or press Dictate and speak; the browser asks for the microphone the first time, and the words land in the same box. Name lines, steps and served parts as the recipe names them: “swap the sweet potato hash for the crispy potatoes on E004”, “drop the wash-and-peel step”, “call it the pre-cut version”. State every number you know: a quantity, a yield, a price, the minutes on a step. Anything you do not state is asked, never guessed.
  3. Press Interpret.

Review the proposal card

  • Facts restate what the instruction said, with no figure you did not state.
  • Four tiles count the line changes, the step changes, the open questions, and the placeholders.
  • What changes, and where each figure came from lists every figure the resolver set, from → to, with its tag and its source: a catalog line and supplier, another recipe’s line, a formula, or your instruction.
  • Open questions come in three kinds:
    • A question to answer: type the answer in its field. It can be waived with Waive, and the figure is then carried from the source recipe tagged Placeholder, with the question as its note.
    • A question that must be answered: which line or step you meant when the name fits more than one. It cannot be waived.
    • A confirmation: the platform found the figures itself, for instance “Crispy potatoes is a served component of AMK-E-004: potato wedges 28 lb, olive oil 0.25 lb per 100-portion batch. Use it in place of Sweet potato hash?” Press Use it and its lines, prices, yields, crediting and prep and cook steps come across, scaled to this recipe’s batch. Type in the field instead to name something else.
  • Add anything else in the follow-up box and press Re-interpret with answers. Your answers and the follow-up are read with the original instruction as one turn; a settled question is not asked again.

Read the comparison

The table appears the moment the proposal is clean and re-resolves live as questions are answered or waived. Both recipes are costed at the same forecast: the same plant and derived batch rule, the same shrink allowance, packaging and distribution.

Keep the variant

Save variant to library writes the variant as a library recipe at status Developing, with its estimated study, and links to it on Recipes. A Developing recipe never enters production planning until its status is changed. Until saved, the variant lives on this page only; Clear proposal discards it.
